How the borrowing-cost comparison works

For each month, estimate the benchmark home's principal-and-interest payment at that month's mortgage rate. Then find the home price with the same payment at the selected reference rate. Both calculations assume 20% down, a fresh 25-year amortization and Canadian fixed-rate semi-annual compounding, with monthly payments.

A higher same-payment price means a greater financing burden relative to the reference rate. This is a modelled payment comparison, not a new market valuation, inflation adjustment or income-based affordability measure. It excludes taxes, maintenance, condo fees, insurance and transaction costs. It does not model the future rate at renewal or an existing owner's outstanding loan.

Prices cover the Toronto area, not all of Ontario. They are nominal sale averages, not a quality-adjusted HPI. The index compares relative changes since the first shared month. For example, 120 means 20% above that month’s value. The tooltip also shows the original percentage and price.

Definitions & data sources

“Arrears” and “delinquency” both mean 90+ days overdue here. The monthly price line breaks at the change in TRREB report coverage around 2024. The plot is limited to the period covered by both series, so the bank forecast stops at the latest available price month. Straight lines connect published observations without inventing intervening data. Rising arrears alone do not establish a house-price bottom.
